# v2.1 design — assimilating drone observations into a physical spread forecast, and a regime signal

## 1. Why a second fire world

v2.0 ended with a forecast that wins on Brier score but over-predicts the affected area about 2.5× (`results/v20_heldout.md`). It also left a structural problem untouched. The 25 m automaton of `world.py` spreads with a free probability, so nothing a drone can measure maps onto any of its parameters. Its forecast can only be re-initialised from the map, never *corrected*.

v2.1 adds the layer that makes correction possible:

1. **A front-scale fire world whose spread rate is a function of measurable inputs.** Rothermel's surface fire model sets the local rate; a level set carries the front (`rothermel.py`, `levelset.py`).
2. **An ensemble Kalman filter** that updates the front *and* the inputs (wind, dead fuel moisture, a rate-of-spread factor) from what the drone sees: perimeter points, local wind and fire radiative power (`assimilation.py`).
3. **A regime signal.** Byram's convection number N_c says whether the kinematic forecast is inside the regime it was built for (`regime.py`).

The question this version answers: *given a drone on station from the alert, does assimilating what it measures make a 15–30 min forecast better than running the same physical model on prior inputs, and better than only correcting the front?*

What it is not: a fire-behaviour model validated on real fires, a plume model, or flight software. Everything is a twin experiment in simulation, with the model errors listed in §3 built into the truth on purpose.

## 2. The front world

### 2.1 Rothermel (`rothermel.py`)

The local rate of spread is that of Rothermel (1972) with Albini's (1976) revisions. The equations are taken from Andrews (2018, RMRS-GTR-371), including the live moisture of extinction and Albini's net-load weights. There is one deliberate departure from the original. Following Andrews, Cruz & Rothermel (2013), the rate is capped at the effective midflame wind speed instead of the original wind limit. Our own convention is added on top: the cap may not push a fire below its no-wind rate.

Fuel models: five of Anderson's (1982) thirteen, the ones that occur in Central European landscapes. They are short grass (1), open timber with grass (2), closed timber litter (8), hardwood or long-needle litter (9) and timber litter with understory (10).

**Check against the published table** (Anderson 1982, table 1, at 8 % dead moisture, 100 % live moisture and 5 mi/h midflame wind). These checks are unit tests in `tests/test_v21.py`.

| fuel model | rate, this code / table | flame length, this code / table |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 27.2 / 26.2 m/min (1.04) | 1.26 / 1.22 m |
| 2 | 10.9 / 11.7 m/min (0.93) | 1.81 / 1.83 m |
| 8 | 0.59 / 0.54 m/min (1.09) | 0.30 / 0.30 m |
| 9 | 2.55 / 2.51 m/min (1.01) | 0.81 / 0.79 m |
| 10 | 2.63 / 2.65 m/min (0.99) | 1.47 / 1.46 m |

The table's values are read off nomographs and rounded, so a ±10 % match is what to expect.

**Wind adjustment factor** (20-ft wind → midflame): 0.4 for the grass models and 0.15 for litter under a closed canopy. Both lie in the ranges of Andrews (2012, RMRS-GTR-266) but are **assumptions**, not site values.

### 2.2 The level set (`levelset.py`)

The fire is {ψ < 0} for a signed distance ψ, and it evolves by

  ∂ψ/∂t + h_W(x, ∇ψ) = 0,

where h_W is the support function of the local fire-spread wavelet W(x). W is the ellipse a point ignition at x would burn in one minute:
- its head rate is Rothermel's, with wind and slope factors added as vectors (Finney 1998);
- its length-to-breadth ratio follows Anderson (1983) at the effective midflame wind;
- the ignition sits at the rear focus (Alexander 1985).

This is Richards' (1990) differential form of the Huygens model that FARSITE uses, written as a level set. The support function is convex and homogeneous of degree one. The Hopf formula therefore makes the exact solution from a point equal to W scaled by t, which is what the unit tests check.

**A formulation that was tried and dropped, with the numbers.** WRF-SFIRE (Mandel, Beezley & Kochanski 2011) drives the front with the wind component normal to it, R(n) = R₀(1 + φ_w(U·n)) + …. That is simpler, but its Hamiltonian is not convex.
- For fuel model 1 at 4 m/s and 6 % moisture, the exact point-ignition head speed of that equation is min_n R(n)/cos∠(n, U) = 8.8 m/min. Rothermel's head rate is 16.5 m/min.
- The first implementation here used an isotropic upwind scheme and returned 14.8 m/min. That is neither number: the scheme was not consistent with the equation it discretised.

Both were measured during development and are the reason for the wavelet form. They are a caution for anyone coupling a normal-component spread rate to a level set: the result depends on the scheme as much as on the physics.

**Numerics.**
- First-order upwind discretisation of the control form, max over v in W of v · ∇ψ. On each quadrant of v it is linear, so its maximum is closed-form; the scheme is monotone for any wavelet.
- Explicit Euler, at 0.8 of the stability limit Δt ≤ Δx / (√2 R_head).
- Re-initialisation to a signed distance every 20 steps. Cells next to the front keep their sub-cell distance ψ/|∇ψ| (Russo & Smereka 2000); all other cells take the distance through the nearest such cell.
- Members are integrated in groups of similar speed, each with its own step and bounding box.

**Checked by unit tests:**
- a planar front moves at exactly R (to 1 %), and the rate-of-spread factor scales it;
- from a point, the head and back distances match the wavelet (head within 2 %; measured: 16.31 vs 16.45 m/min for fuel model 1, 0.560 vs 0.558 for fuel model 9);
- a three-cell non-burnable strip stops a wind-driven grass front;
- re-initialisation keeps a front at a sub-cell position to within 0.5 m.

Cells are 25 m, as in the automaton, so ψ resolves the front position to a fraction of a cell. Fuel boundaries, however, are resolved only to one cell. The ignition is a 60 m disc: first-order schemes cannot carry a sub-cell cone, and by the time a drone maps the fire (minute 10) it is larger than that anyway.

## 3. The truth and its errors

`make_world(seed)` draws a 4 × 4 km landscape (160 × 160 cells) and a fire:

| element | truth | what the forecaster knows |
|---|---|---|
| fuel | five models from a correlated field, weights 0.25 / 0.20 / 0.20 / 0.15 / 0.20 (models 1, 2, 8, 9, 10); 3 % non-burnable patches | the same map with **10 % of cells, in patches, relabelled** to another model |
| terrain | smooth random relief, s.d. 40 m | exact |
| wind | mean 20-ft speed U(1, 7) m/s, random direction; a smooth field of ±15 % speed and ±10° direction; in **40 % of scenarios a shift of 30–70° at minute 20–40** | an NWP-like forecast: speed off by a log-normal factor (s.d. 0.3), direction off by N(0, 25°); no shift |
| dead 1-h moisture | mean U(4, 12) %, smooth field s.d. 1 point | forecast off by N(0, 2 points) |
| rate of spread | Rothermel × a log-normal field (s.d. 0.2, ~100 m correlation): unresolved fuel structure | Rothermel × a scalar factor α, prior log α ~ N(0, 0.3) |
| radiative fraction χ_r | U(0.10, 0.20) per scenario | 0.15 |

The ensemble's spread around the forecast uses the same error statistics as the forecast's own error. Member inputs are bounded:
- wind ≤ 12 m/s;
- dead moisture 2–30 %;
- log α ∈ [−1, 1].

Without these bounds, double log-normal draws produced 30 m/s members that set a 700 m/min CFL limit for the whole ensemble.

## 4. What the drone observes

The drone maps the full perimeter at minute 10 (the alert) and then observes every 5 min until minute 40. It circles the fire, stepping its bearing by 100° per look.

| observation | model | error the filter assumes |
|---|---|---|
| perimeter points | true front points inside a 300 m footprint, at most 30, each displaced by N(0, 10 m) | ψ(point) = 0 ± 15 m |
| local wind | true 20-ft wind at the drone + N(0, 0.7 m/s) per component | ± 1.0 m/s (includes the field's spatial variation) |
| fire radiative power | χ_r × ∫ I_B ds over the true front in the footprint, log-normal noise s.d. 0.2 | log(1 + FRP / 50 kW) ± 0.35 (includes the χ_r uncertainty) |

The FRP operator is what separates moisture from the rate-of-spread factor. Both slow the front, but only moisture also lowers the reaction intensity, so their effects on I_B = I_R · R · t_r differ (Albini 1976). FRP as a measure of combustion rate follows Wooster et al. (2005). **The 0.15 radiative fraction is an assumption.**

## 5. The filter (`assimilation.py`)

The state of each of K = 40 members is ψ (160 × 160) plus the inputs θ = (u, v, m, log α). The analysis is a stochastic EnKF with perturbed observations (Burgers, van Leeuwen & Evensen 1998; Evensen 2009):

  X_a = X_f + ρ∘P_xy (ρ∘P_yy + R)⁻¹ (y + ε − H X_f).

Localisation ρ:
- front cells against perimeter points: Gaspari–Cohn (1999), half-width c, support 2c;
- front cells against wind and FRP: 0;
- inputs against everything: 1, because the inputs are global per member.

After the analysis, ψ is re-initialised. Between analyses, JE's inputs take a random walk (wind 0.4 m/s — 0.8 m/s in the frozen configuration, §7 —, moisture 0.3 points and log α 0.03 per 5 min) so that the filter can follow a wind shift.

Three forecasters see identical prior draws, i.e. paired:

| | front | inputs | observations used |
|---|---|---|---|
| **OL** open loop | mapped at minute 10, then free | prior | none after minute 10 |
| **SO** state only | EnKF from perimeter points | prior | perimeter |
| **JE** joint | EnKF | EnKF + random walk | perimeter, wind, FRP |

SO is the strong baseline. It is what a FARSITE user does by hand: re-draw the perimeter, keep the inputs. JE has to beat it to show that *estimating the inputs* adds anything.

Every forecaster issues forecasts at minutes 20, 30 and 40 for +15 and +30 min. P(affected) is the ensemble fraction with ψ < 0. Scores:
- **the squared error Σ(P − 1_burnt)² summed over the whole domain**, in m² (primary since §7);
- as in v2.0 (`response.forecast_scores`): Brier on the relevant set, the share of truly burnt cells inside the P ≥ 0.1 envelope, and forecast/true area;
- the IoU of the P ≥ 0.5 region.

## 6. The regime signal (`regime.py`)

N_c = 2 g I / (ρ c_p T_a (U − R)³) at each member's head:
- I is the largest Byram intensity on the member's front;
- R is the local front speed there;
- U is the member's 20-ft wind.

The thresholds N_c < 2 (wind-driven) and N_c > 10 (plume-dominated) are from Morvan & Frangieh (2018), as summarised by Accary et al. (2025/26). The signal is:
- **GREEN** if P(wind-driven) ≥ 0.8;
- **RED** if P(plume-dominated) ≥ 0.2;
- **AMBER** otherwise.

Both cut-offs were fixed before any result.

**Stated before testing:** the front world is kinematic, so nothing in it ever *becomes* plume-dominated. What can be tested here is whether the drone's data bring the *estimate* of N_c, and so the regime call, closer to the truth world's value, which is computed from its own head intensity, head rate and local wind. Whether a RED call predicts forecast failure can only be tested on real fires; §9 says how.

## 7. Choices made on screening seeds, and what was seen there

Screening seeds 0–39 are disjoint from the held-out seeds below. Every file is kept in `results/v21_screen_*.jsonl.gz`. Development went through three rounds.

**Round 0** (`r0_*`). The joint filter cut the wind error in half but **lost** to both baselines: on the v2.0 Brier at +30 min it beat SO in only 40 of 120 pairs. One seed, traced cycle by cycle (seed 17), showed two faults:

1. **Inconsistent localisation.** P_yy was localised between perimeter points while the input–observation covariance was not. Distant perimeter points were therefore counted as independent evidence about the same global wind. At minute 15 the ensemble-mean wind went from (3.8, 4.0) to (−1.5, 6.6) m/s, against a direct reading of (0.8, 1.8). **Fix:** front cells use the localised P_yy, the inputs the unlocalised one, i.e. each state row gets the gain that is consistent for it.
2. **An FRP operator that measured geometry, not fire.** The first operator integrated FRP over the footprint, so it mostly reported whether a member's front lay inside the footprint. Predicted 1.6 against observed 5.6 in log units drove log α to its bound. **Fix:** the operator is now FRP per metre of flaming front around the drone, χ_r × mean I_B.

The ablations in round 0 (no FRP, no α, no random walk) are kept for the record. They belong to the faulty code and are not used for anything.

**Round 1 = round 2 base** (`r2_base`): the corrected filter. Here the v2.0 forecast score turned out to be the problem:

| +30 min, 40 seeds × 3 issue times | OL | SO | JE |
|---|---|---|---|
| v2.0 Brier (mean over the forecast's own relevant set), median | 0.105 | 0.101 | 0.108 |
| summed squared error / true area, median | 0.235 | 0.199 | **0.160** |
| IoU of P ≥ 0.5, median | 0.72 | 0.77 | **0.81** |

The two scores give opposite verdicts: on JE against SO, 44:76 by v2.0's Brier and 72:48 by summed squared error. The reason is in the metric. v2.0's Brier is a mean over the cells where *that* forecast gives P ≥ 0.01 or the cell burnt. A wide forecast adds many easy, near-zero cells and so lowers its own mean; a sharper forecast is penalised for being sharp. Because the set depends on the forecast, it is not a proper way to compare two forecasts.

**The primary score is therefore changed, before the held-out run, to the squared error summed over the whole domain.** For a deterministic forecast this equals the misclassified area. It is proper cell by cell and compares forecasts on the same set. v2.0's Brier is still reported, and its direction is itself a prediction (E47). This also affects v2.0's E36, see §10.

**Round 2: two candidate changes, each with its decision rule fixed before it ran.**
- *Per-member model-error fields* (log-normal ROS, s.d. 0.2, the same family as the truth's): **no gain.** On JE's summed error, the variant against the base won 49:71 at +15 and 57:63 at +30. Not adopted.
- *Larger wind random walk, 0.8 instead of 0.4 m/s per 5 min* (rule: better than the base on JE's summed error at +30, and not worse without a wind shift): 75:45 overall and 38:22 without a shift. **Adopted.** Without it, JE beat SO 43:17 in scenarios without a shift but only 29:31 with one; the walk was too slow to follow a shift.

Nothing else was tuned. Fixed a priori, not screened:
- K = 40;
- the localisation half-width of 150 m;
- the observation errors of §4;
- the regime thresholds and traffic-light cut-offs of §6;
- the bounds of §3.

**Numbers seen on screening (base configuration, 120 forecasts per lead):**
- Summed squared error at +30 min: JE < OL in 92 of 120 pairs, JE < SO in 72. At +15 min, SO < OL in 102 of 120.
- Median wind error at the issue times: 2.17 m/s (prior) against 0.91 m/s (JE).
- Median moisture error: 1.14 against 0.83 points (69:51, p = 0.06).
- Median |log₁₀ N_c error|: 0.24 against 0.14 (84:36).
- Truth regimes at the issue times: 115 wind-driven, 5 transitional, 0 plume-dominated.

The predictions below were written after seeing these. As in v2.0, they test whether the screening pattern holds on fresh seeds; they are not blind guesses.

## 8. Protocol

**Held-out seeds, never run before this document was committed:** 21000–21199. That makes 200 scenarios × 3 issue times = 600 paired forecasts per lead time.

**Configuration:** defaults plus `rw_wind_ms = 0.8` (`FROZEN` in `experiments/v21.py`).

**Run:** `python experiments/v21.py heldout`. **Report:** `python experiments/v21.py report`, which uses `experiments/v21_report.py`, committed with this document.

**Statistics:** paired one-sided sign tests (ties dropped) over (scenario × issue time).

**Pre-registered expectations.**

- **E40.** JE beats the open loop: its summed squared error at +30 min is lower than OL's (sign test p < 0.01).
- **E41.** JE beats the state-only filter: summed squared error at +30 min below SO's (p < 0.05). *This is the test that estimating inputs, not just redrawing the perimeter, adds skill.*
- **E42.** Redrawing the perimeter alone already helps at short range: SO < OL at +15 min (p < 0.05).
- **E43.** The drone's wind reading, filtered, beats the NWP-like prior: JE's median wind error at the issue times is ≤ 0.6 × the prior's.
- **E44.** The FRP channel makes dead fuel moisture identifiable: JE's moisture error is below the prior's (p < 0.05).
- **E45.** JE's forecast is calibrated in area: at +30 min its median area ratio lies in [0.8, 1.25] and its P ≥ 0.1 envelope covers ≥ 90 % of truly burnt cells (median). Compare v2.0's ensemble, which over-predicted the area about 2.5×.
- **E46.** The drone's data sharpen the regime estimate: JE's |log₁₀ N_c error| is below the prior's (p < 0.05).
- **E47.** v2.0's relevant-set Brier, applied to the same forecasts, ranks SO above JE at +30 min in a majority of pairs, i.e. the metric artefact of §7 persists on fresh seeds.

**What the outcome changes, fixed now.**
- **E40 and E41 hold:** JE becomes the forecast behind the responder brief for the front world. External material may state the held-out numbers as *simulation, twin experiment, structural errors as listed in §3*, and no more.
- **E40 holds but E41 does not:** the claim is limited to "assimilating the mapped perimeter improves the forecast"; estimating wind and moisture from the drone is not claimed.
- **E46 holds:** the regime light ships as an *estimate*, with the limits of §6. Its decision value (does RED predict forecast failure?) is not claimed until the test in §9 has been run.
- **E47 holds:** v2.0's E36 (ensemble against mean-field, decided on relevant-set Brier) is flagged as decided on an improper aggregate and re-checked with summed squared error as a v2.1.x item. The v2.0 verdict is not changed retroactively.

## 9. The test this simulation cannot run

Whether a RED call predicts that the kinematic forecast will fail can only be tested on fires that really change regime. The plan, not yet run:

1. Take public, instrumented prescribed burns that have time-resolved airborne infrared perimeters and in-situ wind, for example the RxCADRE 2012 data set (Ottmar et al. 2016, *IJWF* 25(1)). Its suitability must be checked first.
2. Run this filter on each burn, issue forecasts and lights, and score the forecasts against the next mapped perimeter.
3. Pre-register beforehand: forecasts issued under RED have a larger median summed squared error than those issued under GREEN.

Until that has been done, the light is a calculated quantity with literature thresholds, not a validated warning.

## 10. Limits stated before testing

- **Twin experiment.** Truth and forecaster share the same equations and numerics. The model error is what §3 builds in: fuel misclassification, unresolved spread variability, a spatially varying wind and moisture, wind shifts and the χ_r uncertainty. Real fires add spotting, crowning, fire–atmosphere coupling and fuel models that fit worse; all of these are absent here.
- **The drone is on station from minute 10 to 40.** That needs continuous coverage, as in v2.0's second duty cycle. Perimeter extraction from thermal imagery is assumed to work: no smoke occlusion, no missed segments. The front-length measurement behind "FRP per metre" is assumed available.
- **25 m cells.** Front positions are sub-cell, but fuel boundaries are not. Fires under about 1 ha are scored on a handful of cells and are noisy.
- **Regime.** In this world 96 % of screening issue times were wind-driven. The regime test is therefore a test of estimation, not of classification.
- **v2.0's E36** used the relevant-set Brier criticised in §7. That is noted here and handled per §8; it is not re-litigated in this version.
