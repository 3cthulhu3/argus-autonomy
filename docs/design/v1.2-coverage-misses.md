# v1.2 design — coverage misses

Status: **design and protocol fixed 2026-10-16 (commit `c796702`), before any selection or held-out mission was flown with the new rules; choice committed in `5e2e691` before the held-out run. Results in §7. Outcome: no rule adopted; C0 stays the default.** Runs before this commit: unit tests on seeds 0–1 and 5000–5011 (implementation checks only).

## 1. The problem

In every configuration tested so far, the dominant remaining error is the **coverage miss**: a real fire is dismissed (CLEAR) while the test statistic never rose above its prior, because the drone had not yet looked where the fire was.

- v1.1, 200 m pixels, held-out seeds: all 4 missed fires were coverage misses ([`v1.1-decoy-discrimination.md`](v1.1-decoy-discrimination.md), §14).
- 400 m pixels: 13 of 13 ([`robustness-viirs-pixel.md`](robustness-viirs-pixel.md), §6).

The fire sits in the **ring**, the band of pixels around the flagged ones, because of geolocation error, satellite latency and spread. The v1.1 CLEAR gate only requires the flagged pixels to be seen, and a larger cue reaches the CLEAR threshold faster, before the ring is searched.

## 2. Root cause and what v1.2 does not do

The root cause is the estimator. H1 places independent per-cell fire mass on the cue, so every cold cue cell counts as evidence against fire anywhere, including in the ring. The principled fix is an object-level H1: one fire whose location follows a prior that includes the ring. That changes the estimator and its tests, and is deferred to v1.3.

v1.2 changes only the **stopping rule**. This is the cheaper remedy, it works with any estimator, and it is directly comparable with v1.1.

## 3. Candidate rules

All variants use M3 with the v1.1 CLEAR gate on the flagged pixels, `steer_clear` = −log 99, and one of the following.

| id | rule | parameter grid |
|---|---|---|
| C0 | v1.1: CLEAR once the flagged pixels are seen | — |
| D*k* | **Dwell.** Once the test would clear and the flagged pixels are seen, the planner searches the ring for *k* minutes. CLEAR only if the log-odds stayed ≤ −log 99 throughout, or earlier if the whole ring has been seen. | *k* ∈ {1, 2, 3, 5} min |
| R*f* | **Ring cover.** CLEAR also requires a fraction *f* of the allowed ring cells to have been seen. While the test would clear, the planner is steered to the unseen ring. | *f* ∈ {0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0} |

R1.0 is the full ring gate. Screened in v1.1 (§12), it left 567 of 1000 decoy missions undecided, and it is included as a reference. The pre-decision policy of each variant does not depend on the thresholds (a, b), so one sweep run per mission yields every threshold pair. Unit test: `test_v12_first_passage_equals_online`.

## 4. Protocol

**Geometries.** G200 (v1.1 satellite model, 200 m pixels) and G400 (400 m pixels, same absolute detection area). A rule must work in both, because the robustness test showed that thresholds do not transfer.

**Seeds.**
- Selection: B 0–999, A and C 0–499.
- Held-out: **fresh** B 2000–2999, A and C 1000–1499, never flown before. The v1.1 held-out seeds are not reused, because their misses were inspected one by one in the robustness analysis, and a rule designed with knowledge of them would be tested on them.

**Step 1: operating point per variant and geometry, on selection seeds.** The v1.1 rule (§13 of the v1.1 doc) over the same 16 × 16 threshold grid:
- feasible if the P_FA upper 95 % bound ≤ 2 % and ≤ 5 % of missions are undecided in B and in A ∪ C;
- among feasible pairs, fewest real fires CLEARed;
- ties broken by median time to correct alert, then median time to CLEAR, then smaller a.

**Step 2: choice of rule, on selection seeds.**
- Only variants feasible in both geometries are eligible.
- Choose the one with the fewest real fires CLEARed, summed over both geometries at their operating points.
- Ties are broken first by the larger of the two geometries' median times to CLEAR, then by the simpler rule, in the order C0 < D (smaller *k* first) < R (smaller *f* first).
- If C0 is chosen, v1.2 reports that no stopping rule tested helps.

**Step 3: held-out.** The chosen rule and C0, each at its own per-geometry operating point, flown on-line on the fresh held-out seeds. The choice is committed before this step.
- Paired comparison on identical seeds: exact McNemar for fires CLEARed and for false alerts; sign test for time to CLEAR.

## 5. Pre-registered expectations

- **E18.** On selection seeds, at least one D or R variant is feasible in both geometries and CLEARs fewer real fires in total than C0.
- **E19.** R1.0 is infeasible in at least one geometry, because more than 5 % of decoy missions are left undecided.
- **E20.** On held-out seeds, the chosen rule CLEARs at most half as many real fires as C0, summed over both geometries, while its false-alert point estimate stays ≤ 2 % in each geometry.
- **E21.** On held-out seeds, the chosen rule's median time to CLEAR exceeds C0's by at most 2.0 min in each geometry.

**Power, stated in advance.** C0 misses on the order of 2–6 fires per 1000 per geometry. A halving of about 8 events cannot reach conventional significance with 2000 fire missions. E20 is judged on counts, and the McNemar p-value is reported next to it, not used to rescue or reject it.

## 6. Limitations

- Same 1.6 km world as v1.1. At 400 m the ring covers up to half of it (robustness note, §5), so ring rules are cheap there in a way they would not be in a larger world.
- Ring search uses the same greedy information-rate planner, restricted to the unseen ring. A dedicated search pattern could do better or worse.
- A stopping rule only changes when the drone may stop. Fires outside the ring are not addressed.

## 7. Results

Selection: [`results/v12_select.md`](../../results/v12_select.md). Held-out: [`results/v12_heldout.md`](../../results/v12_heldout.md).

**Step 1–2 (selection seeds).** Eight of nine variants were feasible in both geometries; R1.0 was not (437 of 1000 decoy missions undecided at 400 m). Summed over both geometries, real fires CLEARed were C0 4, D1 7, D2 7, D3 5, D5 2, R0.25 6, R0.5 2 and **R0.75 0**, so step 2 chose R0.75. Dwelling did not help: no D variant beat C0 by more than 2 fires, and D1–D3 did worse.

**Step 3 (fresh held-out seeds), M3 at each variant's own operating points:**

| | C0, 200 m | R0.75, 200 m | C0, 400 m | R0.75, 400 m |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| thresholds a / b | 6 / −10 | 6 / −8 | 2 / −30 | 2 / −2 |
| false alerts (1000) | 0.9 % | 0.9 % | 0.3 % | 0.3 % |
| real fires CLEARed (1000) | 0 | 0 | 6 | 4 |
| undecided, decoy missions | 2 | 0 | 26 | **57** |
| time to CLEAR a decoy, median | 6.17 min | 6.83 min | 8.17 min | **13.50 min** |
| geofence violations · returns to base | 0 · 2000/2000 | 0 · 2000/2000 | 0 · 2000/2000 | 0 · 2000/2000 |

**Pre-registered expectations:**
- **E18 met.** On selection seeds, R0.75 and R0.5 both CLEARed fewer real fires than C0 (0 and 2 against 4).
- **E19 met.** R1.0 is infeasible at 400 m.
- **E20 not met.** On held-out seeds R0.75 CLEARed 4 real fires against C0's 6, not at most 3. Its false alerts stayed at 0.9 % and 0.3 %.
- **E21 not met.** At 400 m the median time to CLEAR rose by 5.3 min (sign test: slower in 819 of 914 paired missions). At 200 m it rose by 0.67 min.
- In addition, R0.75 at 400 m left **5.7 %** of decoy missions undecided on held-out seeds. That breaks the 5 % bound of the selection rule; on selection seeds the figure had been 3.8 %.

**What happened (post hoc, not pre-registered).** The two rules miss *different* fires: paired at 400 m, C0 alone missed 6 and R0.75 alone missed 4.
- **C0's 6 misses** (5 distinct fires) are all coverage misses. Λ never rose above 0 before the CLEAR, and 19–100 % of each fire lay in the ring at launch. **R0.75 caught every one of them.** The ring rule removes the error it was designed for.
- **R0.75's 4 misses** (2 distinct fires) are of other kinds. One fire lay wholly inside the flagged pixels but was small (13 cells at launch) and was dismissed at Λ ≤ −2. The other was a single cell outside both the cue and the ring, which no ring rule can reach.

**Why R0.75 ran with b = −2: the selection rule rewarded optimism on a zero count.** On selection seeds, 144 threshold pairs for R0.75 at 400 m were feasible with *zero* missed fires, with b ranging from −30 to −2. The tie-break then preferred the fastest CLEAR, which is the most permissive threshold. Zero events in 1000 missions cannot distinguish b = −30 from b = −2, so the procedure picked the threshold whose safety it had not measured, and fresh seeds exposed it. The same flaw sits in the v1.1 selection rule. It did not bite there because the minimum miss count for M3 was 2, not 0, so the tie that decided the threshold was a tie among pairs that had each been observed to fail.

**Decision.** No v1.2 stopping rule is adopted. R0.75 fails the undecided bound on fresh data and costs 5 minutes per decoy at 400 m, and its net gain in missed fires (6 → 4) rests on a threshold the protocol could not vet. C0 remains the default. `ring_cover` and `dwell_ticks` stay in the code, off by default, with their tests.

**What a next attempt must change, before it is run:**
1. **Selection rule.** When the minimum miss count is tied across pairs, prefer the *most conservative* clear threshold among the tied pairs, not the fastest. Or require a margin, for example the tied set's median b. This is a change to the protocol and is pre-registered with the next experiment.
2. **Seeds.** The held-out seeds of this note (B 2000–2999, A and C 1000–1499) are now inspected and cannot serve as held-out again. The next experiment draws fresh ones.
3. **Root cause.** The ring rule removes coverage misses by brute force, at a large time cost. The object-level H1 of §2 attacks the reason they occur, and remains the principled v1.3 step.
