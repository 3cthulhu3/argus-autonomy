# v2.0 design — detection and response around the decision test

## 1. From one layer to three

Up to v1.3 the stack answers one question: *given a satellite cue, is there a spreading fire here?* Two questions sit on either side of it.

- **Before:** where does the cue come from when no satellite has flagged anything?
- **After:** what happens once the drone has said ALERT?

v2.0 adds a layer for each, so the pipeline reads **detect → confirm → respond**. The confirmation layer (v1.1–v1.2b, method M3 with D5) is reused unchanged. Every hook added to `decision.py` defaults to v1.x behaviour: 76 existing tests pass, and 12 randomly drawn v1.2b held-out missions re-flown after the change match the stored per-seed results field for field.

What each layer may claim is fixed here, before any held-out run:

| layer | question | what it is | what it is not |
|---|---|---|---|
| **detect** | when does a fire first get noticed? | one dock, one drone, cue-free patrol in the 2-D simulation | a coverage guarantee; a real sensor model below 25 m |
| **confirm** | is it a spreading fire? | v1.2b, unchanged | — |
| **respond** | what do responders get, and can a drone knock the fire down? | a brief and forecast from the estimator's belief; a closed-form feasibility model of dock-launched direct attack | a suppression simulation; flight software; an airworthiness or EASA argument |

## 2. Detection layer (`detection.py`)

**World.** A `LatentFire` ignites at an unknown fire step. Its ignition cell is drawn from the operator's risk map in proportion to hazard^γ, with γ = 2, at least 6 cells from the dock. The risk map is a smoothed random field. Decoys are placed as in v1.1, scaled with map area. Family **F** has one ignition, uniform in minutes 30–150 of a 4.5 h horizon, plus decoys. Family **N** has decoys only.

**Patrol filter.** `HazardFilter` tracks P(something hot) per cell. Its prediction step adds an ignition hazard, b ← b + (1 − b)·h_i. The hazard rates sum to the true conditional rate of one fire per ignition window. For small b, the belief of an unseen cell is about λ_i × (time since seen). An information-rate planner on this belief is therefore a latency-weighted patrol rule in the sense of Smith, Schwager & Rus (2012). No optimality guarantee is claimed.

**Hand-off.** A cue is raised when the filter's P ≥ 0.95 on a cell outside the static-source register. The drone then runs the decision test from where it is, with the endurance it has left. The test is seeded with a cue map: an 8×8 block at the satellite prior's hotspot level, centred on the hot cell, and a ring of the same width.
- **ALERT** ends the run.
- **CLEAR** registers the hot cells of the block, dilated by one cell, as static sources. The patrol filter ignores readings there from then on, which is the analogue of masking FIRMS static sources before dispatch.
- **UNDECIDED** (endurance ran out): after two undecided hand-offs on the same 8×8 block, the source is registered as *unresolved* and counted.

**Planners.**
- **P0:** lawnmower over the whole geofence, repeated.
- **P1u:** information-rate with a uniform hazard.
- **P1:** information-rate with the risk map as hazard.

**Duty cycle.** Sorties last 30 min and are separated by 30 min on the dock ("1 drone"). The second condition has no ground time ("continuous coverage"). It stands for two drones alternating on one dock, or a battery hot-swap. Only the coverage gap is modelled, not the second airframe.

## 3. Response layer

### 3.1 Brief and forecasts (`response.py`)

At ALERT the H1 filter of the decision test holds P(fire) per cell. From it, `responder_brief` produces:
- the alert position relative to the dock;
- the area with P ≥ 0.5 and the expected area;
- the length and width of the belief blob;
- the heading (downwind compass bearing);
- forecasts of the affected area at +15 and +30 min.

Two forecasts are compared:
- **mean-field:** the estimator's own prediction step, iterated without observations. It is known to under-predict growth because of its source gate (`results/prediction_calibration.md`).
- **ensemble:** K = 200 stochastic fires sampled from the belief and advanced with the estimator's spread kernel. Two assumptions are stated up front: cells are sampled independently, and each sampled cell gets a uniformly random remaining burn time.

A persistence forecast (no growth) is reported as the floor.

### 3.2 Suppression feasibility (`suppression.py`)

The 25 m automaton is too coarse for this question, because one cell is already 625 m² of fire. The module therefore works at front scale.

| quantity | relation | source | status |
|---|---|---|---|
| fire shape | ellipse, ignition at rear focus; LB(U) = 0.936e^{0.2566U} + 0.461e^{−0.1548U} − 0.397, U midflame mph; HB from LB | Anderson 1983 as used in FARSITE; Alexander 1985 | literature; LB(0) = 1 checked by test; capped at 8 |
| acceleration | R(t) = R_ss(1 − e^{−t/τ}) | form as in McAlpine & Wakimoto 1991 | **τ ∈ [5, 30] min is an assumption** |
| intensity, flame | I = H w R, H = 18 600 kJ/kg; FL = 0.0775 I^0.46 | Byram 1959 | literature; **w ∈ [0.3, 1.2] kg/m² assumed** |
| water | critical flow CF₁₀ₘ = 2.72 RoS^0.42 L/s (2 m flame depth) | Penney, Habibi & Cattani 2019 | literature, head fires, Australian forest; applied to the whole perimeter (conservative) |
| knockdown | q_k = CF/m × t_ext | — | **t_ext ∈ [15, 60] s is an assumption** |
| direct attack | FL ≤ 1.2 m | Andrews & Rothermel 1982 | literature; 2.4 m as sensitivity |
| airframe | momentum theory, FM 0.6, 40 L, 12 kg pack at 180 Wh/kg | — | **illustrative**; ~12 min fully loaded against 7–10 min dealer figures, i.e. optimistic |
| race | untreated perimeter grows in proportion to its share; treated perimeter stays out | simplified from Fried & Fried 1996 | no re-ignition is optimistic |

Three cross-checks on q_k (order of magnitude only):
- critical flow × 30 s at R = 2 m/min gives about 3 L per metre of perimeter;
- the heat to vaporise water against the fuel energy of a 1 m deep flaming zone gives 2–7 L/m for η = 0.3–1;
- retardant coverage levels give a few L per metre of line.

The model is a feasibility envelope. Its positive results are upper bounds.

## 4. Choices made on screening seeds, and what was seen there

Screening seeds 900–939 (patrol, families F and N) were flown during development and are disjoint from every held-out seed below. Three things were changed on them:

1. **No spread step in the patrol filter.** With the v1.0 spread step, every untested decoy grew a phantom fire during recharges. The cue then drifted off the source, and 41 of 65 hand-offs ended UNDECIDED (seeds 900–914). The spread question now belongs to the decision test alone.
2. **A-priori thresholds ±log 99 for the hand-off test, with D5.** At `RECOMMENDED["NOMINAL"]`'s clear = −15, a single-block cue's log-odds saturates at about −14.8, so CLEAR is unreachable (seed 913). This repeats the v1.1 robustness finding that operating points do not transfer across cue geometries. A patrol-specific operating point would need its own sweep, which is left for later.
3. **Reference airframe** tuned to about 12 min of fully loaded hover, before any suppression result was computed.

Fixed a priori, not screened:
- cue threshold 0.95 (= v1.0's confirmation threshold);
- the two-undecided rule;
- K = 200;
- all assumption ranges in §3.2 except the midflame wind, narrowed from 0–15 to 0–10 km/h because the uncapped LB reached about 10 at the top of the old range.

Numbers seen on screening seeds:
- **Nominal fire, 64×64, 1 drone, median ignition → ALERT:** P0 45, P1u 33, P1 32 min (40 seeds).
- **Continuous coverage, P1 (20 seeds):** 13 min at 64×64, 7 min at 32×32.
- **Slow fire, P1:** 82 min, against 41 for the nominal fire (20 seeds).
- **Measured head spread rate** of the nominal automaton: about 20 m/min. With one fire step per 10 min (`slow_fire`), about 2 m/min.

The predictions below were written after seeing these. They are therefore tests of whether the screening pattern holds on fresh seeds, not blind guesses.

## 5. Limits stated before testing

- **The patrol drone is not the suppression drone.** The confirming drone carries a camera, not water. A water drone launches from the dock at ALERT.
- **Latency comes from a 25 m automaton.** The coupling to the suppression model uses only each fire's measured alert latency and dock distance, not the automaton's fire size.
- **Airspace is not modelled.** Nothing here addresses BVLOS authorisation, the payload drop, or deconfliction with manned firefighting aircraft. Under EASA, a payload-dropping BVLOS operation is at least in the specific category (SORA).
- **Reload is assumed automated.** The turnaround of 120 s assumes water refill and battery swap at the dock without people.
- **One dock, one fire at a time.** No network of docks is modelled.

## 6. Protocol

**Held-out seeds, never flown before this document was committed:**
- patrol, family F: 10000–10199;
- patrol, family N: 11000–11199 (separate RNG stream from v1.x);
- forecasts: v1.x families A and C, seeds 6000–6199 (unused by any earlier experiment).

**Conditions** (`experiments/v20.py`):
- **D1:** maps 32×32, 48×48, 64×64 (0.64, 1.44, 2.56 km²) × {1 drone, continuous} × {P0, P1u, P1}, nominal fire, family F.
- **D2:** 64×64, 1 drone, family N, all planners.
- **D3:** 64×64 slow fire, {1 drone, continuous} × {P0, P1}, family F.
- **R1:** M3 at `RECOMMENDED["NOMINAL"]`, satellite-cued, with matched wind or the estimator assuming calm air (`WIND_MISMATCH`). Each correct ALERT is scored at +15 and +30 min against the true fire advanced from the same state.
- **R2:** envelope over R_ss ∈ {1, 2, 3} m/min × dock distance × alert latency, 400 draws per cell. End to end: each D3 fire's own latency and dock distance, mean P(contained) over the 200 fires, with never-alerted fires counted as 0.

**Statistics.** Medians count never-alerted fires as ∞, with 2000-sample bootstrap CIs. Paired comparisons use a one-sided sign test (ties dropped). Rates get Clopper–Pearson 95 % intervals.

**Pre-registered expectations.**
- **E30.** At 64×64 with 1 drone, P1 alerts earlier than P0 on paired fires (sign test p < 0.05).
- **E31.** The risk map adds little: P1 vs P1u is not significant (p ≥ 0.05) and the medians differ by less than 3 min.
- **E32.** With 1 drone and a 30-min recharge, the 10-min KPI of the lab plan (median ignition → detection ≤ 10 min, here → correct ALERT) is not met at any map size. With continuous coverage it is met at 0.64 km² only.
- **E33.** On fire-free maps, P1's false ALERTs per hand-off have a 95 % upper bound ≤ 2 %.
- **E34.** Zero geofence violations, and every sortie ends on the dock except the one in progress when a run ends.
- **E35.** Slow fire: P1's median ignition → ALERT is more than 1.5× the nominal fire's (1 drone, 64×64), because growth is the test's evidence.
- **E36.** At +30 min with matched wind, the ensemble forecast's Brier score is lower than mean-field's on paired alerts (sign test p < 0.05).
- **E37.** The ensemble's P ≥ 0.1 envelope covers ≥ 80 % of truly affected cells at +15 min (median, matched wind), and covers less under the calm-air assumption.
- **E38.** End to end at R_ss = 2 m/min, P(contained) is below 0.25 with 1 drone and recharge, and below 0.5 even with continuous coverage and 2 water drones in rotation.
- **E39.** At R_ss = 2 m/min with 1 drone, raising the pack from 180 to 400 Wh/kg raises end-to-end P(contained) by less than 0.10. Latency, not payload, is the binding constraint.

**What the outcome changes, fixed now.** There is no adoption criterion: nothing replaces D5. The consequences of the results are fixed in advance:
- If E38 holds, dock-launched suppression stays a research item. The response layer's product is the brief and the forecast, and any claim of autonomous suppression is withdrawn from external material.
- If E39 holds, the radar trigger "≥ 400 Wh/kg at scale" is downgraded: it would not reopen the suppression thesis by itself.
- If E35 holds, the next confirmation work (v1.4 or later) targets slow-fire evidence other than growth, such as radiometric intensity or smoke.

## 7. Results

Full tables: [`results/v20_heldout.md`](../../results/v20_heldout.md). Runs: `results/v20_patrol_runs.jsonl.gz` (5000 patrols), `results/v20_forecast_runs.jsonl.gz` (800 decision missions).

**Verdicts: 9 of 10 expectations held.**

| | outcome | evidence |
|---|---|---|
| E30 | held | P1 alerts earlier than P0 on 145 of 200 paired fires, later on 50 (p = 3·10⁻¹²); medians 31.2 vs 52.2 min |
| E31 | **not held** | P1 vs P1u is not significant (101:86, p = 0.15), but the medians differ by 5.0 min (31.2 vs 36.2), above the 3-min bound |
| E32 | held | 1 drone: 13.7 / 24.8 / 31.2 min at 0.64 / 1.44 / 2.56 km²; continuous coverage: 7.2 / 12.7 / 16.7 min. The 10-min KPI is met only at 0.64 km² with continuous coverage |
| E33 | held | 1 false ALERT in 558 hand-offs (0.2 %, upper bound 1.0 %) |
| E34 | held | 0 geofence violations in 5000 patrols; every sortie not on the dock at the end of a run was the one in progress |
| E35 | held | slow fire 60.9 min against nominal 31.2 min (×1.95) |
| E36 | held | ensemble Brier lower on 332 of 396 alerts at +30 min (p = 5·10⁻⁴⁵); medians 0.239 vs 0.394 |
| E37 | held | +15 min envelope covers 100 % (matched) and 94 % (calm-air assumption) of truly affected cells |
| E38 | held | R_ss = 2 m/min end to end: 0.02 (1 drone, recharge); 0.08 (continuous coverage, 2 water drones) |
| E39 | held | R_ss = 2 m/min, 1 drone: 0.02 at 180 Wh/kg and 0.02 at 400 Wh/kg |

**What the numbers say (post hoc, not tested):**

1. **Detection latency splits into finding and confirming.**
   - For the nominal fire, the gap from cue to ALERT is about 1 min at every map size. Latency is therefore almost entirely the patrol's revisit time, and it grows roughly linearly with area per dock.
   - The KPI of the lab plan (500 ha, 1–2 stations, detection < 10 min) is not reachable with these parameters. Meeting it would take continuous coverage and about 0.64 km² per dock, i.e. on the order of eight two-drone docks for 500 ha.
   - The parameters here are a 175 m camera swath at 10 m/s. Coverage rate scales with swath × speed, so a wider swath moves this boundary roughly in proportion. That is plausible, not tested.
2. **Slow fires are confirmed slowly, and occasionally dismissed.**
   - With continuous coverage, P1 first touches a slow fire after 28.6 min and confirms it after 40.5 min: about 12 min of confirmation against about 1 min for the nominal fire.
   - Real fires CLEARed rise to 1–3 per 200 (0.5–1.5 %), against 1 in 2000 for D5 under satellite cues.
   - Growth is the test's evidence, and a slow fire supplies little of it. This is exactly the regime in which a drone's payload could matter (item 4).
3. **The ensemble forecast wins on Brier score but is biased high.**
   - The ensemble over-predicts the affected area by about 2.5× with matched wind. Mean-field under-predicts it by about 0.5×.
   - Its envelope is conservative (cover ≈ 1.0), which suits "where not to send a crew" better than "how big will it be".
   - Under the calm-air assumption the ensemble's Brier is *lower* than with matched wind, because the missing wind term partly cancels the over-growth. This is a symptom of the bias, not a virtue of the wrong model.
   - The two likely sources are the independent sampling of boundary cells and the random burn times, i.e. the two stated assumptions. Both are candidates for calibration.
4. **Suppression has a window, and the measured latencies miss it.**
   - In the model, a single 40 L drone contains a 2 m/min fire with probability above 0.5 only if it is alerted within about 10 min and the fire lies within 1 km of the dock. At 1 m/min the window is about 25–30 min.
   - The patrol's measured slow-fire latencies are 40–60 min. End to end, P(contained) is 0.02–0.08 at 2 m/min and 0.20–0.48 at 1 m/min.
   - Pack energy does not move it: payload at these distances is already at the 40 L limit, so time is binding, not energy. Relaxing the flame-length gate to 2.4 m adds at most 0.02.

**Consequences, as fixed in §6:**
- **E38 held.** Dock-launched suppression stays a research item. The response layer's product is the responder brief and the forecast, and external material does not claim autonomous suppression.
- **E39 held.** The radar trigger "≥ 400 Wh/kg at scale" no longer reopens the suppression thesis by itself.
- **E35 held.** The next confirmation work targets slow-fire evidence other than growth.

The model also says what *would* open the window: latency, not energy. Ignition-to-alert must fall below about 10 min for 2 m/min fires, which needs dense docks (item 1) and non-growth evidence (item 2). This is a hypothesis for a later pre-registered test, not a result.
