# v1.3 design — an object-level fire hypothesis

Status: **design fixed 2026-10-18. Protocol (§5) committed in `5a39d99` before any selection or held-out mission with the new estimator was flown; selection committed in `5f8e107` before the held-out run. Outcome (§6): all four expectations met, adoption criterion not met; D5 stays `RECOMMENDED`.** Runs before this commit: unit tests, and the one-parameter screen of §4 on seeds 5000+.

## 1. The cause v1.2 compensated for

Every configuration tested since v1.1 has the same dominant error: the *coverage miss*. A real fire lies just outside the flagged satellite pixel, the drone sees the flagged pixel cold, and it dismisses the alert before it has looked where the fire is ([v1.2](v1.2-coverage-misses.md), §1). v1.2 compensated by brute force: D5 keeps searching the ring for five minutes before any CLEAR. That removed the misses at 400 m pixels, but it costs about 2.5 min per false alarm, and it leaves the cause in place.

The cause is the fire hypothesis H1 of v1.1. H1 places *independent* fire mass on every cue cell (probability 0.25 each). A cold look at any cue cell is therefore evidence against H1 as a whole: the model has no way to say "not here, so perhaps there". A large cue seen cold yields many independent pieces of evidence against fire. That is why larger satellite pixels made dismissal faster and misses more frequent ([robustness note](robustness-viirs-pixel.md), §6).

## 2. The model

H1 becomes *H0 plus one fire object at an unknown location*:

```latex
p(z \mid H_1, \text{past}) = \sum_{j=1}^{J} w_j \, p(z \mid H_{1,j}, \text{past}), \qquad w_j' \propto w_j \, p(z \mid H_{1,j}, \text{past}).
```

- **Component j** is the H0 world, where decoys explain the cue. Added to it is a fire blob: a disc of radius r around centre c_j, with fire probability p_blob inside and the H0 classes scaled by (1 − p_blob). Each component spreads its fire with the v1.0 gated mean-field model and is updated exactly like the v1.1 categorical filter.
- **The centres** tile the cue and its ring on a lattice of spacing s. If the cue misses the lattice, it is widened by one spacing. With no cue at all, the centres tile the grid with uniform weight.
- **The prior weights** are proportional to the satellite map's excess over its background, summed over each blob. Per cell, a cue blob therefore outweighs a ring blob by 0.248 / 0.018 = 13.8. Because the ring has eight times the cue's area, it still carries about a third of the prior mass at 200 m pixels.
- **The consequence.** Outside blob j, component j predicts exactly what H0 predicts. A cold look at the cue therefore lowers the weight of cue components and raises that of ring components; it does not lower H1 as a whole. From cold cue looks alone, the log-odds cannot fall below the log of the unseen ring components' weight. This is tested exactly, up to the 10⁻⁶ probability floor (`test_cold_looks_at_one_blob_cannot_push_h1_below_the_other_blobs_weight`). In effect, the model searches the ring because the ring still holds probability, not because a rule says so.
- **The interface is unchanged** (`MixtureFilter` in [`mixture.py`](../../src/argus_autonomy/mixture.py)), so planner, decision rule, CLEAR gate and safety monitor are identical. With one component, it reproduces the v1.1 filter exactly (`test_single_component_mixture_equals_categorical_filter`).
- **Cost.** J is about 40 components at 200 m and about 170 at 400 m, vectorised. Components below weight 10⁻⁹ are pruned. A mission takes 1–4 s instead of 0.3 s.

## 3. What the model does not fix, stated before testing

- **Fires outside the ring** are outside the prior's support.
- **The cell-independence *within* each component remains.** The overconfidence measured in v1.1 (§14) is reduced only where its cause was the cue-wide fire mass.
- **The cost of dismissal moves rather than vanishes.** To dismiss, the model must now see enough of the ring to bring the ring components down. At 400 m the ring covers up to half the grid, so dismissal may be slower than with D5. The smoke runs on seeds 5000+ suggest this.

## 4. The one free parameter

The geometry fixes r = 3 cells (a 29-cell disc, the size of a typical fire at launch) and s = 4 (spacing below the disc's diameter, so neighbouring discs overlap). p_blob ∈ {0.3, 0.6} was screened on seeds 5000+ under a rule fixed before the run (`experiments/screen_v13_pblob.py`): keep 0.6 unless 0.3 is better by 2 points in both geometries on the frequent failures, false alerts plus undecided. Missed fires are too rare to resolve on 600 missions. Result ([`results/screen_v13_pblob.md`](../../results/screen_v13_pblob.md)): **p_blob = 0.6 kept.** With 0.3, 33 of 200 decoy missions at 400 m were undecided, against 1 with 0.6. The screen also showed O CLEARing 2–3 of 200 real fires at ±log 99. The object model alone does not remove misses at a-priori thresholds. This was seen before the protocol was committed and is recorded here so that E27 is read against it.

## 5. Protocol

**Variants.** All use M3 with the CLEAR gate and steering at −log 99:
- **C0**: v1.1 rule;
- **D5**: the current `RECOMMENDED`;
- **O**: object-level H1, no dwell.

**Geometries:** 200 m and 400 m pixels.

**Selection seeds** (B 0–999, A and C 0–499). O is swept to endurance; C0 and D5 come from the existing v1.2 sweep on the same seeds. The operating point per variant and geometry uses the corrected v1.2b rule:
- feasible if the false-alert upper bound is at most 2 % and at most 3 % of missions are undecided;
- among feasible pairs, fewest real fires CLEARed;
- among ties, the most conservative clear threshold, then the faster alert, the faster CLEAR, the smaller a.

**Held-out seeds, fresh:** B 4000–4999, A and C 2000–2499, never flown. C0, D5 and O are flown on-line at their own operating points. Paired comparisons on identical seeds: exact McNemar for decisions, sign test for times.

**Pre-registered expectations.**
- **E26.** O is feasible in both geometries on selection seeds.
- **E27.** On held-out seeds, O CLEARs no more real fires than D5, summed over both geometries.
- **E28.** On held-out seeds, O's median time to CLEAR a decoy exceeds D5's at 400 m.
- **E29.** On held-out seeds, O's median time to correct alert is within 0.5 min of D5's in both geometries.

**Adoption criterion, fixed now.** O replaces D5 as `RECOMMENDED` only if, on the held-out seeds, all of the following hold:
- (i) at most 5 % undecided in both families and both geometries;
- (ii) false alerts at most 2 % in both geometries;
- (iii) no more real fires CLEARed than D5, summed over both geometries;
- (iv) median time to CLEAR no more than 1 min above D5's in each geometry;
- (v) a strict improvement: fewer real fires CLEARed than D5, or a faster median time to CLEAR in both geometries.

Otherwise D5 stays, and O is kept as the research baseline for v1.4.

**Power, stated in advance.** D5 missed 1 fire in 2000 fire missions in v1.2b. A difference in missed fires between O and D5 is therefore almost certainly unresolvable here. (iii) is a non-inferiority guard, not a test, and adoption will in practice turn on (iv) and the time half of (v).

## 6. Results

Selection: [`results/v13_select.md`](../../results/v13_select.md). Held-out: [`results/v13_heldout.md`](../../results/v13_heldout.md). The O sweep was interrupted once by a container restart at 1,952 of 4,000 missions and resumed. Missions are deterministic per seed; two recovered missions were re-flown and matched exactly.

**Held-out (fresh seeds), each variant at its own operating point:**

| | C0, 200 m | D5, 200 m | **O, 200 m** | C0, 400 m | D5, 400 m | **O, 400 m** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| thresholds a / b | 6 / −15 | 6 / −15 | 3 / −30 | 2 / −30 | 2 / −30 | 2 / −10 |
| false alerts (1000) | 1.4 % | 1.4 % | **0.6 %** | 0.6 % | 0.6 % | **0.2 %** |
| real fires CLEARed (1000) | 4 | 1 | 1 | 3 | 1 | 1 |
| undecided, decoy missions | 15 | 6 | 1 | 16 | 2 | 25 |
| median time to correct alert | 2.17 min | 2.17 min | **1.67 min** | 2.17 min | 2.17 min | **1.83 min** |
| median time to CLEAR a decoy | 7.33 min | 9.50 min | **7.17 min** | 8.17 min | 10.67 min | 14.50 min |
| geofence violations · returns to base | 0 · 2000 | 0 · 2000 | 0 · 2000 | 0 · 2000 | 0 · 2000 | 0 · 2000 |

**Pre-registered expectations: all met.**
- **E26.** O is feasible in both geometries.
- **E27.** O CLEARed 2 real fires against D5's 2, and C0's 7.
- **E28.** At 400 m, O is slower to CLEAR than D5: +3.83 min, slower in 836 and faster in 125 paired missions.
- **E29.** O's alert time is within 0.5 min of D5's. It is in fact faster, by 0.50 and 0.33 min.

**Adoption criterion: not met, so D5 stays `RECOMMENDED`.**
- (i)–(iii) hold.
- (iv) fails: at 400 m, O is 3.83 min slower than D5, above the 1-minute allowance.
- (v) fails: the missed-fire counts are equal, and O is faster in one geometry only.

The criterion was written to judge the two geometries jointly, and it is applied as written.

**What the data show beyond the criterion (post hoc, not pre-registered).**

1. **At 200 m pixels, O is better than D5 on every measured dimension:**
   - CLEAR 2.33 min sooner (faster in 798, slower in 163 of 973 paired missions; sign test p < 10⁻¹⁰⁰);
   - alerts 0.5 min sooner;
   - 1 undecided decoy mission against 6;
   - the same single missed fire;
   - fewer false alerts, 6 against 14. The false-alert difference is not significant (paired: D5 alone 14, O alone 6; exact McNemar p = 0.12).

   A rule that adopted O for fine satellite products and D5 for VIIRS-sized ones would plausibly beat both. It would, however, be a choice made after seeing these data. It needs its own pre-registered test on fresh seeds before it can be claimed.
2. **At 400 m, O's slowness is the cost of its guarantee, as §3 predicted.** The ring covers up to half of the 1.6 km world (robustness note, §5). O does not dismiss until the ring components have lost their weight, which requires looking at most of the ring. D5 stops after five minutes of ring search, whether or not the ring has been covered. This mechanism is plausible but has not been measured: ring coverage at CLEAR was not logged for O.
3. **The one remaining miss is shared, and it is a new kind.** O and D5 both miss the same fire, A 2339, in both geometries. It had 3 burning cells at launch, all in the ring. O's components assume a 29-cell fire with probability 0.6. A 3-cell fire produces far fewer hits than any component predicts, so the evidence weighs against the very component that is right. This is a *model-size mismatch*, not a coverage miss. It points to v1.4: a mixture over fire size as well as location.
4. **C0 against the two ring-aware estimators.** C0 missed 7 fires against 2 each for D5 and O. The paired comparison O vs C0 is 3–0 at 200 m and 2–0 at 400 m. The direction of v1.2 is confirmed on a third set of fresh seeds; the size of the effect is still small in absolute counts.

**Conclusion.** The object-level hypothesis does what it was designed to do. It removes the cause of coverage misses instead of compensating for it, and on fine satellite products it is faster and at least as accurate as the blunt rule. On VIIRS-sized products, the honest version of "have I looked everywhere the fire could be?" takes longer than the blunt rule's fixed search. The decision between them depends on the satellite product a deployment uses. That is a deployment question, and v1.3 has measured its two sides.
