# v1.1 design — decoy discrimination and calibrated confirmation

Status: **design approved 2026-10-14 · milestones (i) and (ii) done** (§10–12) · milestone (iii) done (§13–14) · (iv) open · Scope item 1 of v1.1 (item 2, the reachability-verified return-to-base monitor, is designed separately but constrains §5.3).

## 1. Why: v1.0 cannot tell a fire from a hot rock

The v1.0 sensor model treats false alarms as independent per look (`p_false_alarm = 0.03`, redrawn every observation). A *persistent* non-fire heat source — a sunlit rock, a vehicle, a contained agricultural burn — answers "hot" on every pass, and under an independent-noise model repeated hot answers are exactly the evidence that drives a cell to confirmation.

Measured, not assumed ([`experiments/probe_decoys.py`](../../experiments/probe_decoys.py), nominal scenario, info-rate planner, three static 2×2 decoys outside the satellite cue, ≥ 300 m from the fire):

> **50/100 missions confirm at least one decoy cell as fire (95 % CI 40–60 %, Clopper–Pearson). In 30/100 an entire decoy is confirmed.**

This is a mild setting: the planner had no reason to visit the decoys. When the satellite cue itself is a decoy — the common case operationally, since a dispatch starts from a cue — the rate can only rise.

The obvious patch ("require N hits over time before confirming") makes it worse: **persistence is the decoy's signature, not the fire's.** What distinguishes a fire is that it *spreads*; a decoy does not. Discrimination must therefore exploit dynamics, and optionally a second, more discriminating look.

## 2. Reframed question

> Dispatched on a satellite cue, how quickly can a single UAV decide **ALERT (there is a spreading fire, here)** or **CLEAR (nothing that spreads)**, at a controlled and *measured* false-alert rate?

The output of a mission changes from "a belief map" to **a decision with a time stamp**, followed by a map only if the decision is ALERT. This is the operational product of the confirmation-and-cueing pivot: a responder acts on an alert, not on a heat map.

## 3. World model changes

### 3.1 Latent cell classes

Each cell is in one of four classes:

| class | meaning | spreads | static |
|---|---|---|---|
| ∅ | background | — | yes |
| F | fire-affected (burning or burnt) | **yes** | no (grows) |
| W | warm decoy: sunlit rock, vehicle, roof | no | yes |
| H | hot decoy: contained burn, campfire | no | yes |

H is the hard case by construction: at a single instant it is physically a fire. It differs only in that it does not spread. Counting an alert on H as false is a modelling choice for the "high-risk fire" task (open decision D1, §9).

### 3.2 Scenario generator

Three families, all on the v1.0 grid, wind, fuel and satellite model:

| family | fire | decoys | purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** fire + decoys | yes | N_W ~ Poisson(1.5), N_H ~ Poisson(1.0); 1–2 × 1–2 cells; ≥ 6 cells from the fire at launch | detection and *mislocalisation* |
| **B** decoys only | no | as A, conditioned on ≥ 1 decoy flagged by the satellite | **false-alert rate** — the cue exists, nothing spreads |
| **C** fire only | yes | none | regression against v1.0 |

Decoys enter the satellite overpass like burning cells (H always, W with probability 0.5). Family B rejects samples without a cue, because no cue means no dispatch.

### 3.3 Sensors

**Wide thermal** (unchanged geometry, 7×7 footprint, one tick): binary hit z with class-conditional rate θ_x.

| | ∅ | F | W | H |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P(z = 1 \| x) | 0.03 | 0.85 | 0.70 | 0.85 |

**Close look** (new action): hover/descend over a cell for `t_close = 2` ticks, 3×3 footprint, radiometric reading y ∈ {cold, warm, hot}.

| x \ y | cold | warm | hot |
|---|---|---|---|
| ∅ | 0.90 | 0.08 | 0.02 |
| F | 0.05 | 0.15 | 0.80 |
| W | 0.10 | 0.80 | 0.10 |
| H | 0.05 | 0.15 | 0.80 |

The close look separates W from F well and **F from H not at all** — deliberately, so that the claim stays conservative: the only route to rejecting a contained burn is watching it fail to grow. All numbers are illustrative assumptions, not measurements (§8).

## 4. Estimator: two filters, one test

### 4.1 Categorical filter

Generalise `OccupancyFilter` from binary log-odds to a per-cell categorical belief b_i(x). Update is exact Bayes per cell: b_i'(x) ∝ b_i(x) · p(z_i | x).

Two instances run in parallel:

- **H1 filter** over {∅, F, W, H}. Prediction: the v1.0 gated mean-field spread, applied to the F mass and drawn only from ∅ — with s_j = clip(b_j^F − b_j^F[t − burn_steps], 0, 1) · 𝟙[b_j^F ≥ gate] and π_i = 1 − ∏_j (1 − q_j s_j),
  b_i^F ← b_i^F + b_i^∅ π_i,  b_i^∅ ← b_i^∅ (1 − π_i),  W and H unchanged.
- **H0 filter** over {∅, W, H}. Prediction: identity. A static world.

Priors, from the v1.0 satellite map p_i^sat and background decoy densities ρ_W, ρ_H:
- H1: b_i(F) = p_i^sat, b_i(W) = ρ_W, b_i(H) = ρ_H.
- H0: whatever the satellite saw must be a decoy. With e_i = p_i^sat − p_bg the excess over the satellite background: b_i(W) = ρ_W + ω e_i, b_i(H) = ρ_H + (1 − ω) e_i + p_bg. The background goes to H, whose response equals F's, so a cold look at untouched ground is evidence for neither hypothesis (unit-tested).
- ω is matched to the scenario generator (≈ 0.43). ω = 0 was tried and rejected on data (§10).

**Regression invariant:** restricted to {∅, F} with ρ = 0, the H1 filter must reproduce `OccupancyFilter` to numerical precision. This is the first unit test.

### 4.2 Sequential test on the hypotheses

Before each update, each filter gives the predictive likelihood of the incoming footprint observation (cells independent *within* a model):

  p(z_fp | H_k, past) = ∏_{i ∈ fp} Σ_x b_i^k(x) p(z_i | x).

The posterior log-odds of a fire,

  Λ_t = log(π / (1 − π)) + Σ_{s ≤ t} [log p(z_s | H1, past) − log p(z_s | H0, past)],

is a Bayesian SPRT statistic (Wald 1945). Decision rule:

- **ALERT** at the first t with Λ_t ≥ a; location = belief-weighted centroid of the largest connected component of {b^F ≥ 0.5} in the H1 filter.
- **CLEAR** at the first t with Λ_t ≤ b.
- **UNDECIDED** if endurance runs out first — reported, never silently folded into either.

If the models were exact, Wald's bound would give P(ALERT | H0) ≤ e^{−a} × prior odds. They are not: within-model cell independence and mean-field prediction make Λ overconfident. The thresholds are therefore **set empirically** (§6.3), and the gap between Wald's nominal rate and the measured rate is itself reported as a result.

Why this structure and not a per-cell threshold: the question a responder asks is hypothesis-level ("is there a spreading fire?"), and H0 explains a persistent hot spot without penalty while H1 is penalised every time its predicted spread fails to appear. Growth becomes evidence automatically; no hand-written "is it growing?" rule.

## 5. Planner: active discrimination

### 5.1 Decision phase — Chernoff drift

Before the test stops, the planner maximises the expected *drift of Λ* per tick, the classical rule for active sequential hypothesis testing (Chernoff 1959; Naghshvar & Javidi 2013). Given H, cells are independent, so the expected log-likelihood-ratio increment is additive over the footprint:

  D(c) = P_1 Σ_{i ∈ fp(c)} KL(p_i^1 ‖ p_i^0) + P_0 Σ_{i ∈ fp(c)} KL(p_i^0 ‖ p_i^1),

with p_i^k the predictive distribution of cell i's reading under H_k and P_1 = σ(Λ_t). Score = D(c) / (travel(c) + τ) for a wide look, D_close(c) / (travel(c) + τ + t_close) for a close look; the planner takes the best action over both modes within the safety monitor's feasible set.

Expected behaviour, to be verified rather than assumed: over the cue itself, F and H predict identical readings, so drift there is zero; drift appears at the *edge* of a hot blob once H1 predicts ignition of neighbours that H0 says stay cold. The planner should learn to **watch the edge**. W decoys should be resolved by a close look.

Fallback: when max D is near zero (nothing seen yet), the hypotheses barely differ anywhere; the planner falls back to v1.0 info-rate on P(any heat) to find candidates. Pure Chernoff-type rules are known to need such exploration (Naghshvar & Javidi 2013).

### 5.2 After the decision

- ALERT → mapping phase: v1.0 info-rate on b^F of the H1 filter, unchanged.
- CLEAR → return to base immediately. Endurance saved on a false cue is a reported metric.

### 5.3 Interaction with safety

A close look commits the vehicle for `t_close` ticks. The safety monitor must accept a close look only if return remains feasible after it (`remaining − t_close`). This is the first concrete requirement handed to the v1.1 item 2 design.

## 6. Evaluation protocol

### 6.1 Methods (ablation ladder)

| id | filter | decision | planner | answers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| M0 | v1.0 binary | any cell ≥ 0.95 | info-rate | the baseline failure |
| M1 | two-filter test | SPRT | info-rate | does the *estimator* alone fix it? |
| M2 | two-filter test | SPRT | Chernoff, wide only | does *active* discrimination buy speed? |
| M3 | two-filter test | SPRT | Chernoff + close look | what does the second modality buy? |
| Oracle | decoy classes known | SPRT | Chernoff + close look | lower bound on delay |

M0 never issues CLEAR; for it, "nothing confirmed at landing" is scored as CLEAR at landing time.

### 6.2 Metrics

Per mission: decision ∈ {ALERT, CLEAR, UNDECIDED}, decision time, alert correctness (centroid within 100 m of a truly affected cell), localisation error.

Rates, each with a Clopper–Pearson 95 % interval:
- **P_FA** = P(ALERT | family B) — the headline safety number;
- **P_MIS** = P(ALERT located on a decoy | family A);
- **P_D** = P(correct ALERT within endurance | fire);
- **P_U** = P(UNDECIDED).

Delays: median time to correct alert (A, C) and to CLEAR (B), with bootstrap CIs. Per-dispatch false-alert rate at any base rate π follows without re-running: (1 − π) P_FA + π P_MIS.

Map quality after ALERT (A, C): F1 and perimeter error, as in v1.0.

### 6.3 Threshold sweep at no extra cost

Before stopping, the planner never looks at a or b. So one full-endurance run per seed with the Λ trajectory logged yields the decision for **every** (a, b) as a first-passage time, and the full curve comes from a single run. (After the decision the policy changes, so post-decision metrics are re-run at the chosen operating point only.)

Curve: median time to correct alert against P_FA over the (a, b) grid — a DET-style trade-off curve per method.

**Operating point, chosen on held-out data:** seeds 0–999 select the fastest (a, b) whose P_FA upper 95 % bound is ≤ 2 %; seeds 1000–1999 report it. With zero false alerts in 1000 missions the rule of three bounds P_FA at 0.3 %; the protocol cannot certify anything tighter, and the report says so.

Seed counts: family B 1000 + 1000, families A and C 500 + 500 each, five methods. At ~0.2 s per mission in v1.0 (measured), the full benchmark is minutes, not hours.

### 6.4 Calibration

1. Wald-nominal e^{−a} · π/(1 − π) vs measured P_FA at each threshold — the overconfidence factor of the approximate likelihoods.
2. Reliability diagram: σ(Λ_t) at fixed times, binned, against the true hypothesis frequency.

### 6.5 Pre-registered expectations (reported whether confirmed or not)

- E1. M0's P_FA on family B is ≥ 30 % (the probe suggests so).
- E2. M1 brings P_FA under the 2 % target, at a cost in delay.
- E3. M2's curve dominates M1's: faster at matched P_FA.
- E4. M3 shortens time to CLEAR for W decoys but **not for H decoys**, whose time to CLEAR is set by the fire-spread time scale. If E4 holds, it is the physics floor of the problem — a contained burn and a nascent fire are indistinguishable until the fire grows — and it gets its own paragraph in the report.
- E5. No regression on family C: F1 within 0.01 of v1.0.
- E6 (added 2026-10-14, before the milestone (ii) benchmark was run). With φ = 1, H1 explains the whole cue as fire while H0 assigns 43 % of it to warm decoys, so a *hot* close-look reading from a hot decoy is evidence for fire (≈ 1.7 nats for a 2×2 decoy, by hand). Prediction: M3 with φ = 0.5 has fewer false alerts than M3 with φ = 1. Motivated by the min_drift screen, where low thresholds (more close looks) raised M3's false alerts from 5–9 to 18–20 per 400 (`results/screen_min_drift.md`).

Tests, in the order they should be written:
1. `CategoricalFilter` on {∅, F} with ρ = 0 equals `OccupancyFilter` (regression invariant).
2. Predictive distributions sum to one over outcomes, for both sensors.
3. The H0 filter never moves mass between classes in `predict`.
4. Λ is exactly the sum of predictive log-likelihood differences (recompute from logs).
5. Drift D(c) = 0 when both filters hold identical beliefs.
6. First-passage decisions from a logged trajectory equal an on-line run with the same thresholds.
7. Geofence and return-to-base invariants hold with close looks (0 violations, 100 % returns) over the full benchmark.
8. Same seed → identical fire, decoys and sensor stream for every method.

Milestones: (i) filter and test, tests 1–4, M1 on families B and C; (ii) Chernoff planner and close look, M2/M3; (iii) benchmark, curves, calibration; (iv) report, README, release v1.1.0 under the existing Zenodo concept DOI. Estimate: about three weeks part-time.

## 8. Limitations, stated up front

- Within each model, cells are independent and prediction is mean-field. Λ will be overconfident. §6.3–6.4 measure the damage; they do not remove it.
- Sensor rates and the decoy taxonomy are illustrative assumptions. A real radiometric dataset would replace §3.3; until then the results are about the *method's* behaviour, not field performance.
- One fire per scenario; the hypothesis is "a spreading fire exists" versus "it does not". Multiple simultaneous fires are out of scope.
- Decoys are static. Moving heat sources (vehicles in motion) are out of scope.
- The Chernoff rule is myopic. A non-myopic planner is v1.1 item 3.

## 10. Implementation notes (milestone (i))

Three things changed between design and code. Each was found by a test or a measurement, not by inspection.

1. **Per-class clipping destroyed the class ratios.** v1.0 clips log-odds at ±15. Applied per class against ∅, fire, warm and hot all climb together on a hit, hit the same cap and end at about ⅓ each, so a confirmed fire cell sat at b^F ≈ 0.33 and the map collapsed (seed 2: map F1 0.49 where v1.0 reaches 0.95). The filter now floors every class *probability* at σ(−15) and renormalises; for two classes this is exactly v1.0's clip. Test: `test_hits_preserve_the_ratio_between_classes_with_equal_response`.
2. **History aliasing.** The prediction history held a view into the belief array instead of a copy, so after `burn_steps` predicts the spread source was computed against the wrong time. Caught by the regression invariant over a full mission, not over a short one. Test: `test_categorical_filter_restricted_to_fire_equals_v10_filter`.
3. **ω = 0 was adopted on a smoke test and reverted on the benchmark.** With ω > 0, H0 explains part of the cue with warm decoys, whose hit rate is lower than fire's, so a slightly misplaced cue reading cold becomes evidence against fire. In a 60-seed smoke test, 3 of 120 fire missions were CLEARed with the matched ω and 1 of 120 with ω = 0, which looked decisive. The full benchmark, paired on identical seeds, says otherwise: ω = 0 gives significantly more false alerts (16 vs 1 discordant seeds out of 1000, exact McNemar p < 0.001) and no detectable reduction in missed fires (7 vs 7 discordant across families A and C). The default is the matched ω; ω = 0 stays as an ablation. The lesson is recorded because it generalises: 120 missions cannot resolve a 1 % effect.
4. **Plan on "any heat", not on P(fire).** The wide camera answers "hot or not"; it cannot separate fire from a hot decoy. On a cell no spread model predicted, P(fire) therefore stalls at the prior ratio (≈ 0.78) however often it is seen. The info-rate planner, fed P(fire), computes information as if that uncertainty could be reduced and keeps revisiting confirmed cells: map F1 fell 0.033 below v1.0, worse in 397 of 497 paired seeds. Fed 1 − P(empty), the quantity the sensor resolves, M1's map matches v1.0's (§6.5, E5). The decision still uses the full categorical model; only the planner's input changed.

**Seed hygiene.** Changes 3 and 4 were decided on the benchmark seeds themselves, so the milestone (i) numbers that follow them are mildly optimistic. From milestone (ii) on, free choices are screened on seeds 5000+, and milestone (iii)'s held-out seeds 1000–1999 re-check 3 and 4 as well.

## 11. Milestone (i) results

Full tables: [`results/decoys_m1.md`](../../results/decoys_m1.md). Thresholds at the a-priori ±log 99, not tuned. Percentages with Clopper–Pearson 95 % intervals.

| | M0 (v1.0) | M1 |
|---|---|---|
| B: false alert on a decoy cue (1000 missions) | 100 % (99.6–100) | **2.2 %** (1.4–3.3) |
| A: alert placed on a decoy instead of the fire (500) | 9.8 % (7.3–12.7) | **0.0 %** (0–0.7) |
| A: real fire CLEARed (500) | 0 % | **2.8 %** (1.5–4.7) |
| C: real fire CLEARed (500) | 0 % | 0.4 % (0–1.4) |
| C: time to correct alert, median | 1.83 min | 2.17 min |
| C: map F1 at landing | 0.926 | 0.926 |

The estimator alone removes most of the decoy problem, at the cost of 20 s to the first alert. It does not do so for free: M0 never CLEARs a real fire, because M0 cannot CLEAR at all, while M1 now does in 2.8 % of fire-plus-decoy missions. That is the error that matters operationally.

**Pre-registered expectations (§6.5) so far.** E1 confirmed: M0 alerts on every decoy cue. E2 not met at the a-priori thresholds: P_FA is 2.2 % with an upper bound of 3.3 %, above the 2 % target; whether a threshold pair meets it at an acceptable miss rate is milestone (iii). E5 met: map F1 0.926 vs 0.926, after change 4 in §10. E3 and E4 need milestone (ii).

**Why M1 misses fires** ([`results/decoys_m1_misses.md`](../../results/decoys_m1_misses.md), every miss re-run with the sensor instrumented):

- *Never looked* (9 of 14 misses in family A). The cue covered both the fire and a decoy. The drone resolved the decoy first, watched it fail to spread and CLEARed without a single detection of the fire. H1's per-cell prior commits the whole cue to fire, so one non-spreading blob in the cue becomes evidence against fire *anywhere*. This is a planning and stopping problem: CLEAR should not be allowed while the cue still holds unresolved ground. It is the first thing milestone (ii) must fix, and the Chernoff planner alone will not fix it, because its drift is computed over the hypotheses, not over coverage.
- *Looked, and the fire did not grow as predicted* (5 of 14 in A, 2 of 2 in C). The drone saw the fire (29–228 detections in the family-A cases) and still CLEARed: these fires spread more slowly than the estimator's model, so their slowness read as a decoy's stillness. This is the model-mismatch version of E4's physics floor, and the reason an independent-cell test whose median peak |log-odds| on fire missions is about 2,700–2,850 nats (thresholds: ±4.6) should not be trusted near its thresholds without the calibration of §6.4.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| B: time to CLEAR, median | 4.17 min | 4.50 | 4.50 | 4.50 | 2.67 |

**The CLEAR gate is the result of this milestone.** No CLEAR until every flagged pixel has been seen: missed fires in family A fall from 14 to 3 (11 seeds fixed, none broken; exact McNemar p = 0.001), false alerts are unchanged (22 vs 22, identical seeds), and the cost is time: CLEAR comes later in 415 of 978 paired missions, earlier in none, and the median moves from 4.17 to 4.50 min. Of the 3 remaining misses, 2 saw the fire (34–57 detections) and still cleared it, the slow-fire failure of §11; in 1 (seed 414) all 36 burning cells at launch lay in the ring, none in the flagged pixels. A gate over flagged pixels plus ring missed no fire in the screen (0/500) but left 567/1000 decoy missions undecided at endurance; the gate stops at the flagged pixels on purpose.

**Pre-registered expectations:**

- **E3 not supported.** M2 is indistinguishable from M1: false alerts 23 vs 22 (discordant 5 vs 4), time to CLEAR equal (201 vs 184 seeds faster, p = 0.62). The Chernoff rule is in control in only 8 % of planning calls; the rest falls back to heat search, because the two models disagree by ≥ 2 nats almost nowhere the drone can reach. The screen showed that lowering the threshold does not help M2 either. In this problem, information-rate search for heat already flies to the places where the hypotheses would disagree: the edges of hot blobs are also the cells with the most heat uncertainty. Active hypothesis testing has nothing left to add on top of a good search, at least until the models disagree somewhere the search would not look.
- **E4 confirmed, and stronger than stated.** With warm decoys only in the cue, the close look helps: false alerts 6 → 1 of 349, CLEAR faster in 166 seeds and slower in 81 (p < 10⁻¹⁵), median 4.33 → 3.83 min. With hot decoys only, it does not help *and costs time*: CLEAR slower in 191 seeds and faster in 77 (p < 10⁻¹³), median 4.33 → 4.50 min, false alerts 7 → 9. The physics floor of §6.5 holds: a contained burn and a nascent fire differ only in growth, and no reading, however close, shortens the wait for growth. The model still believes a close look at a hot blob is informative (H0 keeps 43 % of the cue as warm), so M3 spends hover time there. A planner that learned the decoy mix from experience would stop; one that trusts its prior does not.
- **E5 holds.** Map F1 on family C: 0.927–0.929 for M1–M3 against 0.926 for v1.0.
- **E6 refuted.** Prediction: M3 with φ = 0.5 has fewer false alerts than with φ = 1. Measured: more, 26 vs 16 (discordant 18 vs 8, p = 0.08), plus 61 undecided missions and CLEAR 3 min later. The asymmetry the hand calculation found may be real, but it is not what drives false alerts. What φ = 0.5 measurably does is slow everything down: alerts 0.5 min later, CLEAR 3 min later, map F1 0.02–0.03 lower. That is consistent with, though not proof of, a weaker growth prediction: with half the cue's mass on fire, fewer cells pass the spread gate of §4.1. φ = 1 stays.

**The oracle bounds delay, not error.** Knowing every decoy brings CLEAR down to 2.67 min with 0 false alerts in 1000; that 1.8-minute gap to M1–M3 is what decoy ambiguity costs. But the oracle CLEARs 4 real fires in family C, where there are no decoys at all. With decoys known, H0 explains the cue's excess as empty ground, so under cell independence a cold look at a slightly misplaced cue is worth up to about 12 nats against fire (0.24 nats per cold cue cell × 49 cells, by hand), and a few cold looks outvote the first detections of the fire (1–14 per case). The same overconfidence as §11, in a purer form: removing decoy ambiguity exposes it rather than curing it.

**Operational reading.** Recommended configuration for milestone (iii): M3 with the gate. Its 16/1000 false alerts (1.6 %, CI 0.9–2.6 %) are the first point estimate under the 2 % target, but the interval is not; certifying the target is (iii)'s job. Every remaining error, in every method including the oracle, traces back to the same cause: a test statistic built on independent cells, peaking at thousands of nats, decided against a ±4.6 threshold. Milestone (iii) therefore starts with calibration (§6.4), not with threshold tuning.

## 13. Milestone (iii) protocol (fixed 2026-10-14, before any held-out seed was run)

**Sweep mode.** Each mission flies to endurance without deciding and logs (tick, Λ, cue gate open) after every observation. The decision of any pair (a, b) is then a first-passage time: ALERT at the first tick with Λ ≥ a, CLEAR at the first tick with Λ ≤ b and the gate open. This is exact only if the pre-decision policy does not depend on (a, b). In milestones (i)–(ii) one rule did: "the test would CLEAR but the cue is unseen, go and look there" fired at Λ ≤ b. From (iii) on it fires at a fixed `steer_clear` = −log 99, for every threshold pair. Test 6 of §7 (`test_first_passage_from_a_sweep_equals_an_online_run`) checks the equivalence against on-line runs; `steer_clear = None` reproduces milestone (ii) bit for bit (`test_steer_clear_default_leaves_milestone_ii_unchanged`).

**Methods.** M1, M2, M3, all with the CLEAR gate. **Seeds.** Selection: B 0–999, A and C 0–499 (the milestone (i)–(ii) seeds). Held-out: B 1000–1999, A and C 500–999, run once, after the operating points are chosen and committed. **Grid.** a and −b each in {2, 3, log 99, 6, 8, 10, 15, 20, 30, 50, 75, 100, 150, 200, 300, 500} (256 pairs).

**Operating point, per method, on selection seeds only.** Feasible: P_FA upper 95 % bound ≤ 2 % (D3); undecided ≤ 5 % on B and ≤ 5 % on A ∪ C. Among feasible pairs: fewest real fires CLEARed on A ∪ C; ties broken by median time to correct alert (A ∪ C), then median time to CLEAR (B), then smaller a. If no pair is feasible, the report says so and no operating point is claimed.

**Calibration (§6.4).**
1. *Wald gap.* Measured P(Λ ever ≥ a within endurance | B) against the nominal e^{−a} (prior odds 1).
2. *Reliability.* σ(Λ_t) at t = 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 min, selection seeds, B (H0) against A ∪ C (H1), 1000 missions each, so the empirical base rate equals the test's prior of 0.5.
3. *Platt slope.* Logistic fit P(H1 | Λ_t) = σ(α + κ Λ_t) at each t. If Λ were a calibrated log-odds, κ = 1; κ is the factor by which the independent-cell model inflates the evidence. Because scaling Λ by κ is the same as scaling both thresholds by 1/κ, the fit changes no decision; it makes Λ readable as a probability.

**Pre-registered expectations.**
- E7. Λ is overconfident by orders of magnitude: measured P_FA at a = 20 exceeds the nominal e^{−20} ≈ 2 × 10⁻⁹ by a factor ≥ 10⁶, and the Platt slope κ at t = 3 min is below 0.05.
- E8. For M3 a feasible operating point exists on selection seeds, and on held-out seeds its false-alert point estimate is ≤ 2 %. (The held-out upper bound may exceed 2 %; it is reported either way.)
- E9. On held-out seeds, M3 at its operating point CLEARs fewer real fires (A ∪ C) than M3 at ±log 99.

## 14. Milestone (iii) results

Selection: [`results/sweep_select.md`](../../results/sweep_select.md). Held-out: [`results/sweep_heldout.md`](../../results/sweep_heldout.md) and, re-flown on-line at the operating point, [`results/operating_point.md`](../../results/operating_point.md). The protocol (§13) was pushed in commit `b10c5cd` before any sweep ran; the operating points in commit `5391ccb` before any held-out seed ran. The sweep reproduces all 6000 milestone (ii) decisions of M1–M3 at ±log 99 exactly, and the on-line re-flight agrees with the sweep's first-passage decision in 2000 of 2000 held-out missions.

**Operating point.** The rule of §13 picks a = 6, b = −10 for M1 and M3, and a = 6, b = −15 for M2. 91 of 256 pairs were feasible for each method. M3 on held-out seeds, on-line:

| M3, held-out seeds | ±log 99 (a priori) | a = 6, b = −10 (chosen) |
|---|---|---|
| false alerts on a decoy cue (1000) | 1.8 % (1.1–2.8) | **1.2 %** (0.6–2.1) |
| real fire CLEARed (1000, A ∪ C) | 0.2 % (0.0–0.7) | 0.2 % (0.0–0.7) |
| alert in the wrong place (1000) | 0.0 % | 0.0 % |
| undecided (B / A ∪ C) | 0 / 0 | 2 / 0 |
| time to correct alert, median | 2.17 min | 2.17 min |
| time to CLEAR a decoy cue, median | 4.50 min | 6.00 min |
| map F1 at landing, A / C | — | 0.916 / 0.928 |
| geofence violations · returns to base | 0 · 2000/2000 | 0 · 2000/2000 |

The trade is clean: a third fewer false alerts and no slower alerts, paid for with 1.5 minutes more to dismiss a decoy cue. The 2 % target is met in point estimate but not certified: the held-out upper bound is 2.1 %. At a true rate of 1.2 %, about 2000 held-out decoy missions would certify 2 % (upper bound 1.8 % at 24/2000); 1000 cannot.

**Pre-registered expectations:**
- **E7 refuted.** On decoy cues, Λ is overconfident, but by a factor of about 2 at a = log 99 (measured 1.6–2.3 % across M1–M3 against 1 % nominal) and 6–12 at a = 8, not by orders of magnitude; from a = 10 upward no decoy mission ever reached the threshold (0/1000). The Platt slope at 3 min is 0.58 for M3 (0.88 for M1), not below 0.05. It falls with time (0.22 at 5 min) and starts above 1 (1.39 at 1 min, where the prior still dominates). The milestone (ii) diagnosis was half wrong: the thousands of nats are real, but they accumulate on *fire* missions, where they are deserved. The overconfidence that matters sits on the **CLEAR side**: at 3 min, 10 % of missions with Λ ≤ −log 99 actually had a fire (claimed: 0.3 %). The CLEAR gate is what keeps that from turning into missed fires, and it is also why the rule chose b = −10.
- **E8 confirmed, narrowly.** A feasible point exists for M3, and its held-out false-alert estimate is 1.2 % ≤ 2 %. Its upper bound, 2.1 %, is not.
- **E9 refuted.** The operating point CLEARs exactly as many real fires as ±log 99 (2 of 1000 held-out, 2 of 1000 selection). Among pairs that decide, missed fires floor at that count at every false-alert level ([`media/det_curves.png`](../../media/det_curves.png)). Thresholds cannot fix them, because in all four misses (selection A 95, 175; held-out A 684, 687) Λ never rose above 0.7 before the CLEAR and in the sweep, where the drone keeps flying, it passed 100 nats 4–11 min after the CLEAR and ended at 840–1420: the fire became visible only *after* the cue was dismissed. These are coverage misses, not test errors, the kind of seed 414 in §12. A CLEAR is a statement about the flagged pixels, and the report must say so.

**What M1, M2, M3 tell apart at the operating point.** Almost nothing, on held-out seeds: false alerts 1.2 %, 1.3 %, 1.2 %; missed fires 3, 2, 2 of 1000. The estimator and the gate carry the result; the planners do not (E3 in §12).

**Consequences for v1.1.0.** Ship M3 at a = 6, b = −10, `steer_clear` = −log 99. Report the CLEAR as "nothing that spreads in the flagged pixels", not "no fire". The next design question this hands to item 3 (non-myopic planning) is the coverage miss: in the sweep runs, continuing to search for 4–11 minutes after the CLEAR would have caught all four. Whether a CLEAR-then-loiter rule does so without giving back the time saved on decoys is a hypothesis for v1.2, not a result.
