# v1.1 design — decoy discrimination and calibrated confirmation

Status: **design, not yet implemented** · Scope item 1 of v1.1 (item 2, the reachability-verified return-to-base monitor, is designed separately but constrains §5.3).

## 1. Why: v1.0 cannot tell a fire from a hot rock

The v1.0 sensor model treats false alarms as independent per look (`p_false_alarm = 0.03`, redrawn every observation). A *persistent* non-fire heat source — a sunlit rock, a vehicle, a contained agricultural burn — answers "hot" on every pass, and under an independent-noise model repeated hot answers are exactly the evidence that drives a cell to confirmation.

Measured, not assumed ([`experiments/probe_decoys.py`](../../experiments/probe_decoys.py), nominal scenario, info-rate planner, three static 2×2 decoys outside the satellite cue, ≥ 300 m from the fire):

> **50/100 missions confirm at least one decoy cell as fire (95 % CI 40–60 %, Clopper–Pearson). In 30/100 an entire decoy is confirmed.**

This is a mild setting: the planner had no reason to visit the decoys. When the satellite cue itself is a decoy — the common case operationally, since a dispatch starts from a cue — the rate can only rise.

The obvious patch ("require N hits over time before confirming") makes it worse: **persistence is the decoy's signature, not the fire's.** What distinguishes a fire is that it *spreads*; a decoy does not. Discrimination must therefore exploit dynamics, and optionally a second, more discriminating look.

## 2. Reframed question

> Dispatched on a satellite cue, how quickly can a single UAV decide **ALERT (there is a spreading fire, here)** or **CLEAR (nothing that spreads)**, at a controlled and *measured* false-alert rate?

The output of a mission changes from "a belief map" to **a decision with a time stamp**, followed by a map only if the decision is ALERT. This is the operational product of the confirmation-and-cueing pivot: a responder acts on an alert, not on a heat map.

## 3. World model changes

### 3.1 Latent cell classes

Each cell is in one of four classes:

| class | meaning | spreads | static |
|---|---|---|---|
| ∅ | background | — | yes |
| F | fire-affected (burning or burnt) | **yes** | no (grows) |
| W | warm decoy: sunlit rock, vehicle, roof | no | yes |
| H | hot decoy: contained burn, campfire | no | yes |

H is the hard case by construction: at a single instant it is physically a fire. It differs only in that it does not spread. Counting an alert on H as false is a modelling choice for the "high-risk fire" task (open decision D1, §9).

### 3.2 Scenario generator

Three families, all on the v1.0 grid, wind, fuel and satellite model:

| family | fire | decoys | purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** fire + decoys | yes | N_W ~ Poisson(1.5), N_H ~ Poisson(1.0); 1–2 × 1–2 cells; ≥ 6 cells from the fire at launch | detection and *mislocalisation* |
| **B** decoys only | no | as A, conditioned on ≥ 1 decoy flagged by the satellite | **false-alert rate** — the cue exists, nothing spreads |
| **C** fire only | yes | none | regression against v1.0 |

Decoys enter the satellite overpass like burning cells (H always, W with probability 0.5). Family B rejects samples without a cue, because no cue means no dispatch.

### 3.3 Sensors

**Wide thermal** (unchanged geometry, 7×7 footprint, one tick): binary hit z with class-conditional rate θ_x.

| | ∅ | F | W | H |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P(z = 1 \| x) | 0.03 | 0.85 | 0.70 | 0.85 |

**Close look** (new action): hover/descend over a cell for `t_close = 2` ticks, 3×3 footprint, radiometric reading y ∈ {cold, warm, hot}.

| x \ y | cold | warm | hot |
|---|---|---|---|
| ∅ | 0.90 | 0.08 | 0.02 |
| F | 0.05 | 0.15 | 0.80 |
| W | 0.10 | 0.80 | 0.10 |
| H | 0.05 | 0.15 | 0.80 |

The close look separates W from F well and **F from H not at all** — deliberately, so that the claim stays conservative: the only route to rejecting a contained burn is watching it fail to grow. All numbers are illustrative assumptions, not measurements (§8).

## 4. Estimator: two filters, one test

### 4.1 Categorical filter

Generalise `OccupancyFilter` from binary log-odds to a per-cell categorical belief b_i(x). Update is exact Bayes per cell: b_i'(x) ∝ b_i(x) · p(z_i | x).

Two instances run in parallel:

- **H1 filter** over {∅, F, W, H}. Prediction: the v1.0 gated mean-field spread, applied to the F mass and drawn only from ∅ — with s_j = clip(b_j^F − b_j^F[t − burn_steps], 0, 1) · 𝟙[b_j^F ≥ gate] and π_i = 1 − ∏_j (1 − q_j s_j),
  b_i^F ← b_i^F + b_i^∅ π_i,  b_i^∅ ← b_i^∅ (1 − π_i),  W and H unchanged.
- **H0 filter** over {∅, W, H}. Prediction: identity. A static world.

Priors, from the v1.0 satellite map p_i^sat and background decoy densities ρ_W, ρ_H:
- H1: b_i(F) = p_i^sat, b_i(W) = ρ_W, b_i(H) = ρ_H.
- H0: whatever the satellite saw must be a decoy: b_i(W) = ρ_W + ω p_i^sat, b_i(H) = ρ_H + (1 − ω) p_i^sat.

**Regression invariant:** restricted to {∅, F} with ρ = 0, the H1 filter must reproduce `OccupancyFilter` to numerical precision. This is the first unit test.

### 4.2 Sequential test on the hypotheses

Before each update, each filter gives the predictive likelihood of the incoming footprint observation (cells independent *within* a model):

  p(z_fp | H_k, past) = ∏_{i ∈ fp} Σ_x b_i^k(x) p(z_i | x).

The posterior log-odds of a fire,

  Λ_t = log(π / (1 − π)) + Σ_{s ≤ t} [log p(z_s | H1, past) − log p(z_s | H0, past)],

is a Bayesian SPRT statistic (Wald 1945). Decision rule:

- **ALERT** at the first t with Λ_t ≥ a; location = belief-weighted centroid of the largest connected component of {b^F ≥ 0.5} in the H1 filter.
- **CLEAR** at the first t with Λ_t ≤ b.
- **UNDECIDED** if endurance runs out first — reported, never silently folded into either.

If the models were exact, Wald's bound would give P(ALERT | H0) ≤ e^{−a} × prior odds. They are not: within-model cell independence and mean-field prediction make Λ overconfident. The thresholds are therefore **set empirically** (§6.3), and the gap between Wald's nominal rate and the measured rate is itself reported as a result.

Why this structure and not a per-cell threshold: the question a responder asks is hypothesis-level ("is there a spreading fire?"), and H0 explains a persistent hot spot without penalty while H1 is penalised every time its predicted spread fails to appear. Growth becomes evidence automatically; no hand-written "is it growing?" rule.

## 5. Planner: active discrimination

### 5.1 Decision phase — Chernoff drift

Before the test stops, the planner maximises the expected *drift of Λ* per tick, the classical rule for active sequential hypothesis testing (Chernoff 1959; Naghshvar & Javidi 2013). Given H, cells are independent, so the expected log-likelihood-ratio increment is additive over the footprint:

  D(c) = P_1 Σ_{i ∈ fp(c)} KL(p_i^1 ‖ p_i^0) + P_0 Σ_{i ∈ fp(c)} KL(p_i^0 ‖ p_i^1),

with p_i^k the predictive distribution of cell i's reading under H_k and P_1 = σ(Λ_t). Score = D(c) / (travel(c) + τ) for a wide look, D_close(c) / (travel(c) + τ + t_close) for a close look; the planner takes the best action over both modes within the safety monitor's feasible set.

Expected behaviour, to be verified rather than assumed: over the cue itself, F and H predict identical readings, so drift there is zero; drift appears at the *edge* of a hot blob once H1 predicts ignition of neighbours that H0 says stay cold. The planner should learn to **watch the edge**. W decoys should be resolved by a close look.

Fallback: when max D is near zero (nothing seen yet), the hypotheses barely differ anywhere; the planner falls back to v1.0 info-rate on P(any heat) to find candidates. Pure Chernoff-type rules are known to need such exploration (Naghshvar & Javidi 2013).

### 5.2 After the decision

- ALERT → mapping phase: v1.0 info-rate on b^F of the H1 filter, unchanged.
- CLEAR → return to base immediately. Endurance saved on a false cue is a reported metric.

### 5.3 Interaction with safety

A close look commits the vehicle for `t_close` ticks. The safety monitor must accept a close look only if return remains feasible after it (`remaining − t_close`). This is the first concrete requirement handed to the v1.1 item 2 design.

## 6. Evaluation protocol

### 6.1 Methods (ablation ladder)

| id | filter | decision | planner | answers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| M0 | v1.0 binary | any cell ≥ 0.95 | info-rate | the baseline failure |
| M1 | two-filter test | SPRT | info-rate | does the *estimator* alone fix it? |
| M2 | two-filter test | SPRT | Chernoff, wide only | does *active* discrimination buy speed? |
| M3 | two-filter test | SPRT | Chernoff + close look | what does the second modality buy? |
| Oracle | decoy classes known | SPRT | Chernoff + close look | lower bound on delay |

M0 never issues CLEAR; for it, "nothing confirmed at landing" is scored as CLEAR at landing time.

### 6.2 Metrics

Per mission: decision ∈ {ALERT, CLEAR, UNDECIDED}, decision time, alert correctness (centroid within 100 m of a truly affected cell), localisation error.

Rates, each with a Clopper–Pearson 95 % interval:
- **P_FA** = P(ALERT | family B) — the headline safety number;
- **P_MIS** = P(ALERT located on a decoy | family A);
- **P_D** = P(correct ALERT within endurance | fire);
- **P_U** = P(UNDECIDED).

Delays: median time to correct alert (A, C) and to CLEAR (B), with bootstrap CIs. Per-dispatch false-alert rate at any base rate π follows without re-running: (1 − π) P_FA + π P_MIS.

Map quality after ALERT (A, C): F1 and perimeter error, as in v1.0.

### 6.3 Threshold sweep at no extra cost

Before stopping, the planner never looks at a or b. So one full-endurance run per seed with the Λ trajectory logged yields the decision for **every** (a, b) as a first-passage time, and the full curve comes from a single run. (After the decision the policy changes, so post-decision metrics are re-run at the chosen operating point only.)

Curve: median time to correct alert against P_FA over the (a, b) grid — a DET-style trade-off curve per method.

**Operating point, chosen on held-out data:** seeds 0–999 select the fastest (a, b) whose P_FA upper 95 % bound is ≤ 2 %; seeds 1000–1999 report it. With zero false alerts in 1000 missions the rule of three bounds P_FA at 0.3 %; the protocol cannot certify anything tighter, and the report says so.

Seed counts: family B 1000 + 1000, families A and C 500 + 500 each, five methods. At ~0.2 s per mission in v1.0 (measured), the full benchmark is minutes, not hours.

### 6.4 Calibration

1. Wald-nominal e^{−a} · π/(1 − π) vs measured P_FA at each threshold — the overconfidence factor of the approximate likelihoods.
2. Reliability diagram: σ(Λ_t) at fixed times, binned, against the true hypothesis frequency.

### 6.5 Pre-registered expectations (reported whether confirmed or not)

- E1. M0's P_FA on family B is ≥ 30 % (the probe suggests so).
- E2. M1 brings P_FA under the 2 % target, at a cost in delay.
- E3. M2's curve dominates M1's: faster at matched P_FA.
- E4. M3 shortens time to CLEAR for W decoys but **not for H decoys**, whose time to CLEAR is set by the fire-spread time scale. If E4 holds, it is the physics floor of the problem — a contained burn and a nascent fire are indistinguishable until the fire grows — and it gets its own paragraph in the report.
- E5. No regression on family C: F1 within 0.01 of v1.0.

| `metrics.py` | decision metrics; first-passage DET curves from logged Λ |
| `experiments/` | `probe_decoys.py` (done), `run_decoy_benchmark.py`, `make_det_curves.py` |
| `tests/` | see below |
| report, README | new section; "Where it breaks" extended |

Tests, in the order they should be written:
1. `CategoricalFilter` on {∅, F} with ρ = 0 equals `OccupancyFilter` (regression invariant).
2. Predictive distributions sum to one over outcomes, for both sensors.
3. The H0 filter never moves mass between classes in `predict`.
4. Λ is exactly the sum of predictive log-likelihood differences (recompute from logs).
5. Drift D(c) = 0 when both filters hold identical beliefs.
6. First-passage decisions from a logged trajectory equal an on-line run with the same thresholds.
7. Geofence and return-to-base invariants hold with close looks (0 violations, 100 % returns) over the full benchmark.
8. Same seed → identical fire, decoys and sensor stream for every method.

Milestones: (i) filter and test, tests 1–4, M1 on families B and C; (ii) Chernoff planner and close look, M2/M3; (iii) benchmark, curves, calibration; (iv) report, README, release v1.1.0 under the existing Zenodo concept DOI. Estimate: about three weeks part-time.

## 8. Limitations, stated up front

- Within each model, cells are independent and prediction is mean-field. Λ will be overconfident. §6.3–6.4 measure the damage; they do not remove it.
- Sensor rates and the decoy taxonomy are illustrative assumptions. A real radiometric dataset would replace §3.3; until then the results are about the *method's* behaviour, not field performance.
- One fire per scenario; the hypothesis is "a spreading fire exists" versus "it does not". Multiple simultaneous fires are out of scope.
- Decoys are static. Moving heat sources (vehicles in motion) are out of scope.
- The Chernoff rule is myopic. A non-myopic planner is v1.1 item 3.

## 9. Open decisions

| | question | recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | Does an alert on a contained burn (H) count as false? | Yes for the headline, with P_MIS by decoy class reported separately |
| D2 | Should the close look separate F from H at all (smoke-plume extent, say)? | No. Keep them identical so the result cannot be accused of a convenient sensor |
| D3 | Target P_FA | 2 % per no-fire dispatch; the seed budget certifies down to about 0.3 % |
| D4 | Close look in v1.1 or deferred? | Include. Without it, M2 vs M3 — the value of a second modality — is unanswerable |
